- **Framework v0.1.0 → v1.0.0 (boundary change, 2026-05-26):** ARCHITECTURE.md
  §6 previously read "Quarterly. Human-only. Agent-assisted scoring is
  explicitly forbidden because it launders the practice's accountability
  through the same class of system the practice studies." On 2026-05-26 this
  was relaxed: the agent is now permitted to draft predictions, review them
  before merge, and produce retrospective scoring on the same terms as the
  author. Related anti-goals and "Notes for human reviewer" wording were
  reframed across `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`, `TODO.md`, `signals/TEMPLATE.md`,
  `scripts/draft_digest.py`, `scripts/notify_due.py`, and the draft-prediction
  workflow.

  *Rationale recorded for future-self:* the §6 "laundering" worry was
  procedural — it assumed the scorer's identity provided the falsifiability
  guarantee. On reflection, the guarantee comes from the *mechanism* of the
  schema: the falsifier and predicted shape are frozen at creation, and the
  immutability rule (preserved as a v1.0.0 hard constraint) means no scorer
  can rewrite what the prediction said. Whether `human`, `agent`, or `mixed`
  reads the outcome against that frozen statement, the test is the same. The
  scorer field on each retrospective entry preserves the audit trail.

  *What this is not:* it is not a relaxation of §7 boundaries (no specific
  incidents, no attribution, no offensive tooling, no private signals, no
  monetization), and it is not a relaxation of the immutability rule. Those
  rails bind agent and human equally. The change is solely about who is
  allowed to *operate* the framework, not what the framework will produce.

  Per §8, a boundary change is a major version bump: framework 0.1.0 → 1.0.0.
  The repo milestone version (TODO.md) remains unchanged — that tracks
  scaffolding milestones, not framework semantics. The 2026-Q2 retrospective
  itself was compiled on 2026-05-18 before this change, by human only — the
  scorer-field convention applies prospectively to subsequent retrospectives.

## Open questions surfaced this quarter

- **Multi-pattern incidents:** The Fast16/Stuxnet retrospective analysis
  (week 21 digest) touched both ideology-faith-nation (state-directed sabotage)
  and craft-and-peer-recognition (academic publication of the technique).
  The framework currently assigns one pattern per prediction. Whether to allow
  multi-pattern predictions, or to require the human author to pick the primary
  motivation, is unresolved. ARCHITECTURE.md §9 notes this as an open question.

- **arXiv volume:** The cs.CR feed returns 40–50 entries per weekday. Capping
  at 50 entries per source means roughly one week of arXiv papers per digest.
  This is probably the right ratio, but it means fast-moving conference weeks
  (NeurIPS, CCS) will be undersampled. Consider a burst allowance for
  high-submission weeks in a future version.

- **Dedup scope:** Current dedup is per-signal-ID (URL or GUID). The same
  story covered by multiple sources (e.g., Krebs + THN + SANS) produces
  three separate signals with different IDs. The LLM clusters them naturally,
  but the raw signal count is inflated. A title-similarity dedup pass could
  reduce noise, at the cost of complexity. Deferred to v0.8.0 if it proves
  problematic in practice.

## Honest assessment

The practice survived the five-week manual phase without feeling empty.
Writing five predictions against seven patterns surfaced which patterns are
currently most activated (boredom-with-asymmetric-leverage, ideology-faith-nation)
and which have no predictions yet (curiosity-past-the-fence, coercion-and-desperation).
That asymmetry is informative: it reflects the author's current attention, not
the framework's limits, and is worth correcting in Q3.

The automation build (v0.4.0–v0.6.0) took one session and produced a working
pipeline. The first live digest (2026-W21) identified three candidate pattern
activations with honest uncertainty ratings. Whether those activations hold
over the next several weeks will be the first real test of whether the signal
sources are well-chosen.

The practice should continue. The risk at this stage is not that it becomes
useless — it's that the weekly digest becomes a box to check rather than a
thing to read. The checklist on the weekly PR is the guard against that.
